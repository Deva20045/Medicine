## Detailed Page-by-Page Source Content Mapping

### Chapter 41: Thyroid Function Tests (p902–904)
- **Book p902 — Hormone Levels, Disorders & RTH (MED-C41-001 to 013, 13 Qs)**:
  - T4 maximum (10 x T3).
  - Carrier proteins: TBG 70%, Albumin 20%, Transthyretin 10%.
  - Hyperthyroxinemia classification:
    - Thyrotoxicosis (↑ Free T3 / Free T4) → Overworking gland (Hyperthyroidism) vs Normal gland (Stored hormones released / Hormone supplement).
    - Euthyroid hyperthyroxinemia (↑ Total T3 / Total T4) → Causes: 1) TBG excess (OCPs, estrogen, pregnancy, steroids, chronic active hepatitis, inherited rare TBG excess); 2) Drugs inhibiting deiodinase-2; 3) Sick euthyroid syndrome; 4) RTH.
  - RTH Pathogenesis: Partial/completely resistant T3/T4 receptors → uninhibited ↑ TSH → ↑ T3, T4 → No action.
  - Clinical features of RTH: Euthyroid (most common); Toxicosis (rare, due to resistance to TR-β forms).
  - MCT8 defect: Impaired entry of T3 to neuron → Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome (severe cognitive impairment).
  - CNS transport schematic: OATP1C1 + MCT8 at blood-brain barrier → Astrocyte (D2 converts T4 to T3) → Neuron (via MCT8 to T3-responsive gene).

- **Book p903 — Scintigraphy & Metabolic Actions (MED-C41-014 to 026, 13 Qs)**:
  - Technetium-99m (Tc^99m) scan showing ectopic thyroid (no uptake in normal location; intense uptake in subhyoid ectopic location).
  - BMR maintenance: Hypothyroidism (↓ BMR), Hyperthyroidism (↑ BMR).
  - Cardiac actions (α-receptors): ↑ CO, ↑ O2 utilization (except brain & gonads), ↑ heart rate. Toxicosis → ↑ CO; Hyperthyroidism → ↑ peripheral resistance → ↑ BP.
  - Uncoupler of oxidative phosphorylation → Thermogenesis; severe lack in myxedema coma → Hypothermia.
  - Carbohydrate metabolism: 1) Gluconeogenesis, 2) Glucose uptake into cells (Hypothyroidism causes insulin resistance).
  - Fat metabolism: 1) Lipolysis (minimal), 2) Cholesterol clearance (Hypothyroidism causes hypercholesterolemia).
  - Protein metabolism: In severe toxicosis, potent catabolic action → Proteolysis → myopathy, Osteopenia → osteoporosis, Hypercalcemia → hypercalciuria → stones.
  - CNS myelination, β-carotene conversion to Vitamin A, erythropoiesis stimulation.
  - Fertility maintenance: Hypothyroidism → menorrhagia → oligo/amenorrhea → infertility.

- **Book p904 — Drugs & TFT, Testing & Lab Interpretations (MED-C41-027 to 038, 12 Qs)**:
  - Drugs & TFT:
    - Deiodinase inhibitors: Steroids, Propranolol, Iodine, Amiodarone, Propylthiouracil.
    - Enzyme inducers (↑ hormone metabolism): Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Rifampicin, Sertraline, Ritonavir.
    - Causing hypothyroidism: Lithium.
    - Decreasing hormone absorption: Al(OH)3, Cholestyramine, Calcium carbonate, Ferrous sulfate.
  - Testing rules: Always test fT3, fT4, and TSH (0.5–5 mIU/L by 4th-gen CLIA); do not test in critically ill patients.
  - Full 7-row interpretation grid:
    1. Subclinical 1° hypothyroidism: TSH ↑, T4 N, FT4 N, T3 N, FT3 N.
    2. Primary hypothyroidism: TSH ↑, T4 ↓, FT4 ↓, T3 ↓, FT3 ↓ (m/c autoimmune, Hashimoto).
    3. RTH (Euthyroid) / TSH-secreting adenoma (Toxic features): TSH ↑, T4 ↑, FT4 ↑, T3 ↑, FT3 ↑.
    4. Hypothalamo-pituitary: TSH N/↓, T4 ↓, FT4 ↓, T3 ↓, FT3 ↓.
    5. Thyrotoxicosis: TSH ↓, T4 N, FT4 ↑, T3 N, FT3 ↑ (Overworking gland: Graves; Stored hormone: Thyroiditis, Hydatidiform mole/pregnancy).
    6. Sick euthyroid syndrome: TSH N, T4 N, FT4 N, rT3 ↑, FT3 ↓.
    7. T3 toxicosis: TSH ↓, T4 N, FT4 N, T3 N, FT3 ↑.
  - Bottom notes: ↓ TBG in Cirrhosis; serum thyroglobulin ↑ in all thyrotoxicosis except thyrotoxicosis factitia.

### Chapter 42: Hypothyroidism (p905–909)
- **Book p905 — Classification & Causes (MED-C42-001 to 013, 13 Qs)**:
  - Primary hypothyroidism: 1) Autoimmune / Hashimoto (m/c), APS-1/2 association (check for APS-2 before starting L-thyroxine to avoid acute adrenal crisis/shock); 2) Drugs: Lithium (a/w nephrogenic DI), IFN-α (irreversible thyroid destruction vs reversible flu-like syndrome), Amiodarone (hypo and hyper); 3) Iodine deficiency, post-surgical.
  - Secondary hypothyroidism: Pituitary cause, no significant hypothyroidism alone, mass effect (m/c lymphocytic hypophysitis).
  - Consumptive hypothyroidism: Hemangioma with ↑ deiodinase-III expression → ↓ hormones (↑ reverse T3).
  - Congenital hypothyroidism: Mostly thyroid dysgenesis; features: open posterior fontanelle, delay in passing meconium, prolonged physiological jaundice, very little crying, excessive sleep.

- **Book p906 — Hashimoto's Thyroiditis Features & Histology (MED-C42-014 to 025, 12 Qs)**:
  - Features: m/c cause of primary hypothyroidism, autoimmune, most common in middle-aged females.
  - Genetics: HLA-DR3, DR4, DR5 (can be ANA +ve); CTLA-4 / PTPN-22 polymorphisms.
  - Gland changes: Commonly not enlarged; when goiter present: irregular, firm, heterogeneous, non-tender.
  - Gross & micro: Fibrosed pale gland (lymphoid infiltration), lymphocytic infiltration with germinal centers, hypereosinophilic Hürthle cells, absence of colloid, oxyphilic metaplasia.
  - Associated malignancy: Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (MALT lymphoma) > papillary carcinoma of thyroid.
  - Pathological mediators: CD8 and CD4 T-cells, IFN-γ, plasma cells.

- **Book p907 — Presentation, Signs & Complications (MED-C42-026 to 038, 13 Qs)**:
  - Generalized slowing (↓ BMR): Fatigue/tiredness (m/c), slow speech/gait/bowel (constipation)/memory/heart rate, weight gain with poor appetite, delayed DTR, cold intolerance, menorrhagia, infertility.
  - TSH elevation stimulating fibroblasts → matrix GAG accumulation trapping water: facial puffiness, periorbital edema, macroglossia causing obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), hoarse voice, non-pitting edema.
  - Young female signs: Hair loss, irregular cycles, dry skin, mood swings.
  - Note: False-positive hypothyroidism in Addison's disease.
  - Hoffman syndrome: Pseudohypertrophy in calf muscles.
  - Complications: Myxedema coma, Hashimoto encephalopathy, bradycardia, polyserositis (pleural/pericardial effusion, ascites), OSA, CTS (m/c cause in females: hypothyroidism; in males: amyloidosis).

- **Book p908 — Hashimoto Workup & L-Thyroxine Dosing (MED-C42-039 to 050, 12 Qs)**:
  - Confirming Hashimoto's: Autoantibodies negative → transient thyroiditis (postviral/post-vaccine); positive → Hashimoto's: TSH-R Ab (10–20%), TPO Ab (90–100%), Thyroglobulin Ab (80–90%).
  - Treatment cut-offs: Confirmed Hashimoto with TSH > 10 mcu/L; TSH 5–10 mcu/L treated if vague symptoms + TPO +ve, family history, pregnancy, or severe symptoms.
  - L-thyroxine dosing: Age < 60 + symptomatic: 1.6–1.8 µg/kg/day (approx 100 µg/day), titrated by 25–50 µg/day; Elderly (> 60): 25 µg/day (due to cardiotoxicity), titrated by 25 µg/day.

- **Book p909 — Administration, Myxedema Coma & SREAT (MED-C42-051 to 063, 13 Qs)**:
  - Administration: Empty stomach (morning 7–7:30 am) with a glass of water, 30 min before meals. Avoid FeSO4, CaCO3, Al(OH)3, Sucralfate.
  - Follow-up: 3 months TFT review (slow taper if normal). Treatment failure m/c due to non-compliance. Clinical relief 3–6 months after TSH normalizes.
  - Myxedema coma (40% mortality): Triggers: Non-compliance, infections (COVID, LRI). Features: Decreased consciousness ± seizures, bradycardia, low-voltage ECG, Hypo tetrad (hypoventilation, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hypothermia). Protocol: IV L-thyroxine 500 µg OD → 50–100 µg/day, steroids, IV T3 5–20 µg, hypertonic saline (strictly avoid hypotonic saline).
  - SREAT (Hashimoto encephalopathy): Euthyroid status, autoimmune vasculitis unrelated to thyroid function, marked anti-enolase antibodies, HLA-B8. Presentations: progressive cognitive decline, seizures/myoclonus, stroke-like episodes. Treatment: Steroids.

### Chapter 43: Thyrotoxicosis and Thyroiditis (p910–916)
- **Book p910 — Classification, Graves & Iodine Effects (MED-C43-001 to 012, 12 Qs)**:
  - Thyrotoxicosis (↑ free hormones): Overworking gland (Graves m/c, toxic adenoma, toxic MNG, TSH adenoma, struma ovarii) vs Normal gland (Thyroiditis, factitia, amiodarone).
  - Graves features: 75% of thyrotoxicosis, age 20–50, F > M. Associations: Smoking, postpartum, HAART, HLA-DR2, CTLA-4, PTPN-22.
  - Wolff-Chaikoff effect: Excess iodide transiently inhibits synthesis for 5–7 days, followed by escape; seen with amiodarone.
  - Jod-Basedow phenomenon: Iodine supply induces thyrotoxicosis in nodular gland or TRAb-positive patients in iodine-deficient areas.

- **Book p911 — Antibodies, Manifestations & Systemic Features (MED-C43-013 to 024, 12 Qs)**:
  - Antibodies: TRAb (80–95%, most important in Graves), Tg Ab (50–70%), TPO Ab (50–80%).
  - TRAb drives T-cell/fibroblast/GAG activation producing unique Graves triad: Ophthalmopathy (proptosis, lid retraction), Dermopathy (pretibial myxedema), Acropachy.
  - Systemic features: Restlessness, irritability, tremors, tachycardia, sweating, diarrhea, polyuria, warm moist skin, oligomenorrhea/amenorrhea.
  - Elderly: Apathetic thyrotoxicosis (fatigue, weight loss), atrial fibrillation, heart failure worsening.
  - Protein catabolism: Proximal myopathy without fasciculations (fasciculations indicate anterior horn cell disease), bone pain/fractures, hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria/stones.
  - Periodic paralysis resembling hypokalemic paralysis. Contrast note: Weight loss + good appetite = Graves; Weight gain + loss of appetite = Hypothyroid. Endocrine Society of India guidelines.

- **Book p912 — Scintigraphy, IM SLOW & Eye Signs (MED-C43-025 to 036, 12 Qs)**:
  - Scintigraphy table: Normal (25% symmetric, normal salivary); Thyroiditis (low/absent thyroid, prominent salivary); Toxic adenoma (hot nodule, contralateral suppressed, reduced salivary); Graves (75–80% diffuse uptake, reduced salivary).
  - Histology: Tall columnar cells with resorbed colloid (scalloped appearance).
  - Ophthalmopathy: 30% of hyperthyroid population. 40/40/20 rule: 40% develop later, 40% present at diagnosis, 20% initial presentation (U/L → B/L).
  - Extraocular muscle progression mnemonic 'IM SLOW': Inferior rectus → Medial rectus → Superior rectus → Obliques.
  - Contraindication: Radioactive iodine. Treatment: Stop smoking, high-dose IV steroids.
  - Eye signs: Von Graefe (lid lag in down gaze), Joffroy (absent forehead creases on superior gaze), Stellwag (infrequent/incomplete blinking), Dalrymple (widened palpebral fissure during fixation), Möbius (convergence defect).
  - Non-palpable gland = exogenous cause or thyroiditis. Flowchart: TSH ↓, FT3/FT4 ↑, T3/T4 > 20 → TRAb +ve → Graves.

- **Book p913 — Ancillary Labs, Thionamides, RAI & Surgery (MED-C43-037 to 049, 13 Qs)**:
  - Labs: Hyperglycemia, hypercalcemia, leukocytosis, elevated ALP, abnormal LFTs.
  - Remission: 60–70% remission with ATDs in 12–18 months.
  - Thionamide dose equivalence: Methimazole 6 mg = Carbimazole 10 mg = PTU 100 mg.
  - Drug specifics: PTU preferred in pregnancy (↑ protein binding), hepatotoxic; Carbimazole preferred over PTU (5 mg TDS daily, t1/2 6–8 h); Methimazole teratogenic (aplasia cutis, choanal atresia), irreversible inhibitor, t1/2 6 h.
  - Side effects: Rash (m/c), Agranulocytosis (most dangerous), cholestasis, drug-induced lupus.
  - Monitoring: Revisit in 4–6 weeks, monitor free T3 and free T4. Propranolol: 40 mg Q6H.
  - RAI in relapse: Minimum 1 month antithyroid drug pretreatment to avoid crisis; no cancer risk; C/I in pregnancy; worsens eye disease; 10% need lifelong L-thyroxine. Severe eye disease: IV steroids + Rituximab ± radiotherapy.
  - Surgery indications: Suspicion of malignancy, hyperparathyroidism, pregnancy/lactation, young children, compressive symptoms.

- **Book p914 — Thyrotoxic Crisis & Thyroiditis Types (MED-C43-050 to 061, 12 Qs)**:
  - Thyrotoxic crisis: Triggers: Inadequate pretreatment before RAI/surgery, acute illness. Signs: Arrhythmia, fever with delirium, seizures. Protocol: PTU 1000 mg stat → 250 mg Q4H + Steroids + Propranolol 60 mg Q4H + Oral iodine (iopanoic acid 500 mg BD for Wolff-Chaikoff effect).
  - Acute thyroiditis: Post-infection in children, staph/strep from piriform sinus fistula, pain, normal TFT; radiation/trauma induced.
  - Subacute thyroiditis (de Quervain): Granulomatous, post-viral, age 30–50, F > M, HLA-B35, no scintigraphy uptake.
  - Chronic Riedel's thyroiditis: IgG4-related disease, painless hard thyroid, dense fibrosis. Postpartum thyroiditis.

- **Book p915 — Subacute Timeline & Postpartum Comparison (MED-C43-062 to 073, 12 Qs)**:
  - Subacute mechanism: Stored hormone release (↑ FT3/FT4, ↓ TSH, T3/T4 < 15) → Fever > 2 weeks, neck/ear pain, marked ↑↑ ESR → Stored hormone exhausted (15% permanent hypothyroidism) → Recovery. DOC: Aspirin 600 mg Q6H, NSAIDs, steroids.
  - Triphasic timeline graph: 0–6 weeks (toxic phase), 6–12 weeks (hypothyroid phase), 12–18 weeks (recovery).
  - TSH notes: High TSH in 1° hypo, RTH, TSH adenoma, thyroiditis recovery; Normal TSH in central hypothyroidism.
  - Postpartum thyroiditis: Painless/silent, associated with SLE, Sjogren's, T1DM, vitiligo.
  - Comparison table: Subacute has pain, no pregnancy link, non-autoimmune, ↑↑ ESR, -ve TPO, steroids effective (Yes); Postpartum is silent, postpartum timing, autoimmune, normal ESR, +ve TPO, no steroids (β-blockers used).

- **Book p916 — Amiodarone Effects & Summary Grid (MED-C43-074 to 086, 13 Qs)**:
  - Amiodarone hypothyroidism: Transient, due to Wolff-Chaikoff phenomenon.
  - Amiodarone thyrotoxicosis Type I: Underlying Graves/MNG, Jod-Basedow mechanism, ↑↑ Doppler vascularity; stop amiodarone, treat with Thionamides (DOC).
  - Amiodarone thyrotoxicosis Type II: No underlying disease, lysosomal-mediated destruction, no antibodies, ↓↓ Doppler vascularity; stop drug, treat with Steroids.
  - Full 3-column differential grid (Subacute Thyroiditis vs Graves vs Toxic MNG):
    - Duration: Days to weeks vs 2–3 months vs Months to years.
    - Age/sex: Anytime vs 20–40 F>M vs Elderly F>M.
    - Symptoms: Very severe vs Severe vs Mild to moderate.
    - Neck pain: Present vs Absent vs Absent.
    - Goiter: Small vs Diffuse vs Nodular.
    - Eye/Skin signs: Absent vs Present vs Absent.
    - TFT: T3 (N)/↑ vs T3 ↑↑ vs T3 ↑↑.
    - T3/T4 ratio: < 15 vs > 20 vs > 20.
    - TRAb: Absent vs Present vs Absent.
    - Ultrasound / Doppler: Not significant (↓ vascularity) vs Diffuse enlargement (↑↑ vascularity, best test) vs Enlarged nodules (variable vascularity).
    - RAI uptake: Low uptake vs ↑↑ uptake vs Depends on nodule functional status (hot/cold).
